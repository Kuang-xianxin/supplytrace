# Three-minute demonstration

All timestamps are a recording plan, not evidence that a video exists.

0:00–0:20 — Show SupplyTrace and the proposed promise: 30 units of LAMP-01 on 26 October. The fixture is original synthetic data as of 22 October. Explain the user: a purchasing or operations colleague answering a delivery-date question.

0:20–0:55 — Choose AI planning, run the four-source query, then show the result. A valid plan can only reorder four approved reads. The program computes capacity from stock, safety stock, existing commitments and usable shipment dates. The model does not write SQL or choose the final number.

0:55–1:25 — Expand a Zetaris query. Show the source table, query, row IDs, retrieval time and hash. Show the confirmed inbound arriving on the 25th but becoming usable on the 27th after handling and buffer. The 26th only supports 15 units. Unconfirmed inbound and cancelled orders do not increase capacity.

1:25–1:55 — Change only the proposed date to the 28th and run again. Capacity becomes 55. Open the Meterless history and audit event. The earlier decision remains traceable while the current calculation reads current source evidence again.

1:55–2:30 — Open the CSV review workbench's labeled UCI example. Compare the two original quantity values and source positions, generate neutral questions, and show that the application leaves the human note unchanged. These CSV rows are a separate example, not the procurement fixture or live customer inventory.

2:30–3:00 — Export evidence, point to the repository and explain the next pilot: measure review quality and elapsed time with real operators. Current delivery is one local workspace. No customer savings, production reliability or autonomous buying is claimed.

Use continuous browser capture with a visible pointer tied to actual pointer input. Keep queries and responses in chronological order. Do not replace a failed integration with local fixture output or edit the resulting evidence. Captions may explain the action, but must not obscure the data or imply a user study.
